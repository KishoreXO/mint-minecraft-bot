===============================================================================
 1. WHAT THE BOT IS
===============================================================================

A Minecraft survival bot for Java Edition 1.21.9. It joins a LAN world as a
normal player named Mint (change it with BOT_NAME in .env) and plays survival by itself, from empty hands to a
full diamond kit (diamond pickaxe, sword, helmet, chestplate, leggings and
boots). Nobody steers it.

It is built on:
  - Mineflayer, a JavaScript library that plays as a Minecraft client;
  - mineflayer-pathfinder for walking routes;
  - plugins for combat (custom-pvp), finding who hit it (bloodhound), picking
    tools (tool), wearing armour (armor-manager) and eating (auto-eat);
  - Jev from TypeSafe, a fast AI model that answers typed questions with a
    confidence score. It is used only for real judgement calls (section 5).

The rule it is built on: anything that has one right answer (walking to a tree,
crafting a pickaxe, loading a furnace) is ordinary code. Jev is asked only where
the answer depends on judgement, such as whether to fight a mob.


===============================================================================
 2. THE GOAL AND THE EIGHT PHASES
===============================================================================

The bot works through a fixed list of phases (src/progression.js). Each has a
"done" test, and it moves on when that test passes. Some also have a looser
"kept" test so the bot does not slip back a phase the moment it spends one
item. Example: eating its sixth steak underground does not send it back to the
surface for food.

   #  Phase          Done when                                    Numbers from
   1  Wood           32 planks' worth of wood (a log counts as 4)   the trip's needs
   2  Stone tools    stone (or better) pickaxe, sword and axe       gear.js
   3  Food           6 pieces of food in the bag                    the trip's needs
   4  Ready to dig   everything the deep trip needs: food, wood,    mine.js
                     40 blocks to place, a working pickaxe, a
                     weapon
   5  Iron           all the iron the iron kit still needs is mined gear.js
   6  Iron kit       iron pickaxe, sword and armour made            gear.js
   7  Diamonds       29 diamonds collected or already crafted       mine.js
   8  Diamond kit    all six diamond pieces made                    progression.js

Wood first, then the full stone kit, then food, is the order you asked for.
Hunting needs a stone (or better) weapon, unless food is down to 6 or below.

Each number is stored once and reused everywhere. For example, the wood target
IS the deep trip's plank requirement, not a copy of it. When two copies of a
number drifted apart, the bot used to gather too little and stall.

What may skip a phase:
  - anything that keeps the bot alive (priority 50 and up) is never held back;
  - upkeep always runs: picking up drops, cooking, crafting what it can craft
    now, keeping some wood, climbing out for supplies;
  - a "cheap win" can be taken out of order: ore it needs, or an animal,
    within 6 blocks.
Nothing else skips a phase.


===============================================================================
 3. HOW IT DECIDES WHAT TO DO (THE DIRECTOR)
===============================================================================

One scheduler, src/director.js, owns the body. Only one behavior runs at a time.

  - Every behavior has a priority number and a shouldRun() test.
  - The director runs the highest-priority behavior whose shouldRun() is true.
  - A behavior does one small piece of work (chop one log, craft one item, win
    one fight) and returns. Then the director chooses again. This keeps it
    reacting to change all the time.
  - Interrupting: behaviors at priority 50 and above can cut into a running
    one. The running behavior is stopped through a cancel token.
  - Staying on task: the behavior that just did real work gets a small bonus
    for 10 seconds, so the bot finishes jobs instead of flicking between two
    similar ones. The bonus never lets routine work block an emergency.
  - No-op backoff: a behavior that is picked but keeps achieving nothing is
    put on hold for a while, so it cannot hog the bot. "idle" is exempt.
  - Time limit: a behavior that runs over 60 seconds is stopped.
  - All walking goes through src/nav.js, which keeps one active route and
    always releases the movement keys afterwards. A stuck "forward" key once
    made the bot look like it was flying.

Two layers sit next to the director (src/layers/):
  - Reflex layer: checks lava, drowning, a hostile within 3 blocks, low health
    and a big drop on EVERY game tick (20 times a second). When one fires it
    tells the director at once. It can also place a block under the bot's feet
    when it is about to fall into a deep hole.
  - State machine: labels what the bot is doing (idle, gathering, mining,
    crafting, fighting, fleeing) for the status line and the dashboard. It only
    watches; it does not steer.

===============================================================================
 5. JEV: WHERE THE AI MODEL IS USED
===============================================================================

Jev is asked three kinds of question (src/jevClient.js):

  1. Threat: fight, flee or ignore this mob? It is told health, hunger,
     weapon, armour, how many hostiles are around, whether the mob can be
     outrun, and whether it shoots.
  2. Ore: is this ore worth the detour, given what is already in the bag?
  3. Strategy: which need to focus on now (food, tools, mining ...). If the
     answer is at least 35% confident, the matching behaviors get a boost for
     30 seconds. The dashboard shows whether that advice was applied.

The bot never waits on Jev:
  - Jev answers in about 0.3 to 3.6 seconds, but reflexes must happen in
    milliseconds. So src/prefetch.js asks early: every hostile in range and
    every ore type in view gets its question sent while the bot is still
    walking there. The answer is usually waiting when needed.
  - Every question has a deadline: 300 ms for a fight, 400 ms for ore, and 0
    for a mob within 5 blocks or right after being hit. If Jev hasn't answered
    by then, a built-in "instinct" rule decides. The late answer is still
    saved and used next time.
  - An answer below 35% confidence counts as no answer, and instinct decides.
  - A request that times out during a laptop freeze is marked "during a
    stall", so it isn't blamed on Jev.
  - The dashboard and the "Jev" status line show how many decisions Jev made
    versus instinct, and the success rate.


===============================================================================
 6. STAYING ALIVE: FIGHTS, NIGHT, FOOD, HAZARDS
===============================================================================

FIGHTING (behaviors/threat.js, src/tactics.js, src/combat.js)
  - It defends; it doesn't hunt mobs for fun. It reacts to what hurt it, what
    is on top of it, or what shoots at it with a clear line. Everything else is
    ignored while it works.
  - Mob-specific tactics:
      zombies, husks, drowned: builds up 2 blocks and hits down;
      skeletons and other archers: with a bow, uses cover and peeks out to
        shoot; without one, closes in fast (running away just gives them free
        shots);
      creepers: never hit. It walks away every time;
      bosses (wither, warden, ender dragon, elder guardian, ravager, evoker):
        never targets; it only leaves;
      everything else: hits from the edge of its reach, where most mobs can't
        hit back.
  - Switches target mid-fight to a creeper within 6 blocks, or to whatever
    starts hitting it.
  - Stops chasing 18 blocks from where the fight began.
  - Holds off on mobs it can't reach, for longer each time.
  - Flees only from what it can outrun and what doesn't shoot. It knows which
    mobs those are.
  - Works out the world's difficulty from how hard mobs hit (src/difficulty.js).
    Until it has seen enough hits it assumes Hard, the safe guess. "diff" in
    chat shows the result.
  - Watches damage and names its source (src/damage.js). The dashboard and
    the log show damage for this life and for the whole session.

NIGHT
  - bed (92): sleeps when it can. Wool comes from sheep it hunts anyway.
  - shelter (91): with no bed and not strong enough, it digs in and seals the
    roof. It takes its crafting table with it only when it can see it, and
    checks the table actually reached the bag.

FOOD (src/eating.js, auto-eat plugin)
  - Eats whenever there is health to heal, so hunger stays at 18 or more and
    health regenerates. Saves food when already at full health.
  - Raw meat is banned while there is cooked food; raw is allowed only below
    10 hunger with nothing better. Poison and golden apples are never eaten
    automatically.
  - Rotten flesh is the last resort: eaten only at 6 hunger or less with
    nothing else in the bag, because it is better than starving to death.
    It keeps up to 8 for that reason.
  - Also eats carrots, potatoes, beetroot, berries, melon and apples, and
    picks ripe crops, berry bushes and melons while searching for food.
  - Cooks in a smoker (twice as fast) when it can build one.
  - Stops hunting once 3 raw pieces are waiting for the furnace.
  - Auto-eat is paused while it places blocks, crafts, or escapes lava or
    drowning, so food can't be swapped into its hand at the wrong moment.

HAZARDS (src/lava.js, src/falls.js, src/layers/reflex.js)
  - Never digs a block that has lava on any of its six sides.
  - Every manual step refuses lava at foot, head or floor level, and any drop
    deeper than 4 blocks.
  - Pathfinder uses the same 4-block drop limit. Water counts as a safe
    landing only within that same limit, so it doesn't jump into deep water
    from high up.
  - Never digs out from under sand or gravel.
  - Places a block underfoot when it is about to fall into a deep hole.

DEATH AND LOOT (behaviors/loot.js)
  - After respawning it walks back for its items, in 40-block legs, but only
    when it can get there before the 5-minute despawn.
  - The log line "Bot died" records the cause and the damage taken in that
    life.


===============================================================================
 7. WATER AND SWIMMING  (src/water.js, behaviors/survive.js)
===============================================================================

All of this is based on Minecraft's physics, measured in a simulation:
  - In water only the facing direction steers. Looking up or down, sprinting
    and sneaking do nothing useful.
  - Holding jump rises (up to 3.5 blocks/s). With no input the bot sinks
    slowly (0.5 blocks/s).
  - It can climb out onto a bank at most 1 block above the water's surface. A
    bank 2 blocks up can never be climbed.
  - It cannot build a pillar while floating: it bobs up and down too much.
  - Digging underwater is very slow: stone with a stone pickaxe takes 14 s
    floating, 2.85 s standing on the bottom, 0.6 s on dry land.

The swimming controller ("pilot") takes over the movement keys whenever the bot
is in water, and updates them 20 times a second:
  - When nothing else is steering, it holds jump so the bot's head stays above
    water. The exception is a deliberate dive with enough air.
  - It follows planned routes: along the surface to the shore, or in 3D to the
    nearest air.
  - It climbs out: faces the bank and holds forward and jump until it is
    standing on dry ground.

ESCAPE DROWNING (95)
  - Starts when air is low (8 bubbles or fewer out of 20), or at once when the
    bot is under a ceiling, where waiting would only waste air.
  - Takes the swim route to air when it fits the air left, with 50% spare.
  - Otherwise digs up toward air if the dig time fits. It sinks to the bottom
    first when digging from there is faster, and never digs under sand or
    gravel.
  - If neither fits, it still goes for the nearest air rather than waiting.

LEAVE WATER (85). Tries these in order:
  1. Under the surface or under a ceiling: swims up and out first.
  2. Swims to a bank it can actually climb (up to 40 blocks away) and climbs
     out.
  3. No climbable bank, as in a ravine: places a block against the wall,
     climbs onto it, builds up to the top, then steps off onto the edge.
  4. No blocks: cuts steps into the wall with the pickaxe.
  5. Otherwise keeps swimming, or holds at the surface, and tries again. It
     never just stands still.

STAYING OUT
  - A ravine it had to build its way out of is remembered as a "water trap"
    (up to 10 per world). Routes through it are made expensive, and trees,
    drops and animals inside it are ignored.
  - "unstick" and "shelter" leave water to leaveWater. They used to fight it
    for control.
  - Going back into water within 30 s of getting out logs "Re-entered water",
    with the behavior that caused it.


===============================================================================
 8. GATHERING, CRAFTING AND SMELTING
===============================================================================

  - Wood: searches 16, then 32, then 48 blocks. Remembers trees it couldn't
    reach and chops from the base of the trunk.
  - Stone: aims for 40 cobblestone. Only takes stone whose drop it can pick
    up (not stone inside a wall or on a cave ceiling). Granite, diorite,
    andesite and tuff don't count, because they don't drop cobblestone.
  - Pickups: stands close enough (about 2.4 blocks) that drops land at its
    feet, and walks to where a drop actually fell.
  - Crafting (behaviors/gear.js): wooden tools, then stone, iron and diamond;
    a backup pickaxe once the current one is half worn; armour; a shield;
    torches when fullbright is off. When a craft fails, the log says why: not
    enough planks or sticks, not enough material, or interrupted.
  - Stations (src/stations.js): places a crafting table and furnace when
    needed and reuses one within 8 blocks. It gives up on a station only
    after 3 failed trips there.
  - Smelting (behaviors/smelt.js): picks the furnace, smoker (food) or blast
    furnace (ore), loads it, and leaves. A later visit collects the result.
    Fuel is sized to the job, cheapest first, coal last.


===============================================================================
 9. MINING AND GOING DEEP  (behaviors/mine.js)
===============================================================================

Ore sits at particular heights, so the bot mines where it actually is:

   Ore                Best height (Y)   Worth mining between
   Diamond, redstone        -59              -64 ... 14
   Gold                     -16              -64 ... 32
   Lapis                      0              -32 ... 32
   Iron                      16              -24 ... 56
   Copper                    48              -16 ... 112
   Coal                      96                0 ... 190

  - goDeep: digs a staircase down to the right height. It won't go for
    diamonds without an iron pickaxe (stone breaks diamond ore for nothing).
  - stripMine: 12-block tunnels, plus caves nearby (it leaves a cave once
    it has been searched or is crowded with mobs).
  - It mines the whole vein once it finds one.
  - It skips ore more than 2 blocks above it (that caused an up-down loop).
  - Before going deep it needs the whole trip kit (phase 4). Once
    underground it climbs back up only when it is truly out of food or wood.
  - X-ray is off by default: it only mines ore it could really see (an
    exposed face, or one its own tunnels uncover). Section 14.
  - Night shift: once sealed into its night shelter it keeps digging its
    staircase down toward the ore it needs, instead of waiting for dawn.
    When the staircase is refused at a spot (for example boxed in by water),
    it doesn't try again within 2 blocks of that spot. It used to retry 256
    times.


===============================================================================
 10. WHAT IT REMEMBERS (FILES IT WRITES)
===============================================================================

  known-base.json     Where its crafting table and furnace are, and which
                      world it is in (worked out from the host and spawn
                      point).
  world-memory.json   A notebook for each world: the deepest point reached,
                      ore it has seen, where it died, structures, when it
                      last slept, and water traps. A different world gets a
                      fresh notebook automatically.
  stats.json          History across worlds and attempts: how long each
                      milestone took, deaths by cause, the leaderboard. Shown
                      on the dashboard's History tab. "npm run stats" rebuilds
                      it from every log.
  bot-status.txt      A one-page snapshot, rewritten every second.
  logs\session-*.log  Everything that happened, one file per run.

"npm run fresh" deletes the first two and bot-status.txt, then starts the bot.


===============================================================================
 11. THE DASHBOARD WEBSITE  (http://localhost:3000)
===============================================================================

It can only watch (nothing on it controls the bot), and only this computer can
open it.

LIVE VIEW (key 1)
  - 3D feed: the world as the bot sees it, with its planned route and the
    block it is digging drawn in. Buttons: ORBIT, BOT'S EYES, 3D OFF (key 3).
    The view reloads by itself after a respawn or reconnect. Its frame rate
    shows in the corner. It draws at a capped resolution
    (MC_VIEWER_PIXEL_RATIO, default 1) and runs in its own browser process,
    so the rest of the page can't slow it down.
  - Top bar: health, food, air, time of day, ping, Jev success rate, freeze
    count, and a LIVE / RELINKING / NO SIGNAL light. If no update arrives for
    2 seconds the page dims, so old data never looks live.
  - Chips over the 3D view: what it is doing, nearby threats, and the water
    state (in water, surfacing for air, climbing out, building a landing,
    pillaring out).
  - PROGRESSION: the 8 phases, the current one, and an estimated time.
    SUPPLIES underneath shows have/need for wood, cobblestone, food, coal,
    iron and diamonds.
  - SPLITS: how long each phase took this life.
  - RADAR: mobs, animals and players within 32 blocks, the bot's trail, death
    spots with their cause, and water traps.
  - BACKPACK: the inventory with item icons.
  - DECISION STREAM: a story feed of what just happened, with filters. Water
    and air events are included.
  - BRAIN: every behavior the director is weighing, its effective priority
    (boosted ones are marked), and whether it wants to run, is blocked, or
    threw an error.
  - JEV: recent decisions, focus advice and whether it was applied, and calls,
    failures and failures during freezes for each kind of question.
  - Death card: when the bot dies, a replay of its last 10 seconds.
    Esc closes it.
  - Toasts and optional sounds (key S) for big moments.

HISTORY VIEW (key 2)
  "How good is the bot?": a milestone funnel from a fresh start, a world
  leaderboard, deaths by cause, and the time to a stone pickaxe for each
  fresh life.

OTHER
  - /?overlay=1 : a see-through heads-up display for OBS. No death card.
  - The "keys" checkbox turns the single-key shortcuts off.
  - A hidden tab stops streaming after 1 minute, to save the computer's memory,
    and catches up without duplicates when shown again.
  - Built for screen readers: labels, dialog focus, and readable contrast.

quiet. While dead it answers only status, report and where.


===============================================================================
 13. LOGS AND THE LOG SUMMARY
===============================================================================

Each run writes logs\session-<start time>.log: one JSON line per event, with
the time in UTC (India is 5 h 30 min ahead). "npm run logs" summarises the
newest one:

  SIGNALS   bugs that used to happen, counted. They should all be 0. Water
            rows: back in water within 30 s, unstick straight after water,
            leaveWater giving up within 1 s, pillar attempts in water,
            drownings and drowning damage.
  NIGHTS    what it did at night: legs mined, crafts, reasons for not mining.
  PHASES    phases finished and how long each took.
  JEV       decided by Jev, by instinct, answered in advance, failures (and how
            many were during freezes).
  STALLS    moments the program froze for over 400 ms. "not our code" means
            the computer was out of memory.
  FAILED / BACKOFF / OVERRUN   what failed, what was put on hold, what ran
            too long.
  AVOIDABLE DAMAGE and DEATHS   what hurt it and what killed it.

Useful log lines to search for:
  "Bot died", "Stuck in water — getting out", "Reached dry land",
  "Built a landing in the water", "Climbed out of the water",
  "Re-entered water", "Running out of air — surfacing", "Breathing again",
  "Engaging", "Fleeing", "Event loop stalled", "Chat command failed".


===============================================================================
 14. SETTINGS AND CHEATS
===============================================================================

All settings live in .env; the full list is in HOW-TO-RUN.txt section 9.

FULLBRIGHT (on by default)
  A bot has no screen, so it could always "see" in the dark. What fullbright
  changes is that it no longer needs torches before going deep. Without it the
  bot must first find coal and craft torches.

XRAY (off by default, and it really is a cheat)
  The bot can read every block in loaded chunks, including ore hidden in
  rock. With x-ray off it only mines ore a player could see. With it on it
  tunnels straight to hidden ore up to XRAY_RADIUS blocks away. Every lava
  and fall check still applies.

Both can be switched in chat while it runs. The current setting is printed at
startup.

 15. TESTS
===============================================================================

  npm test      35 suites, about 870 checks. No Minecraft needed.
  npx eslint .  code style and mistakes; must be clean.

Some of what they cover:
  - every module loads, and every behavior works on an empty bot;
  - mob types, tool choice, eating rules, lava, falls, staircases;
  - the whole path from empty hands to a diamond kit, so a stuck threshold
    fails a test instead of wasting an hour of play;

    sealed pocket, and the ravine climb to y=78.

===============================================================================
 17. KNOWN LIMITS
===============================================================================

  - It still dies sometimes, mostly to skeletons' arrows and to melee at night.
  - Swimming and water escapes are tested against real game physics in
    test/waterSim.test.js. Check "npm run logs" after a session near water.
  - On a laptop with little free memory the bot can freeze for a moment
    ("Event loop stalled ... not our code" in the log). Turning the 3D view
    off helps the most.
  - Pathfinder cannot plan routes through water, so the bot's own water code
    does that.
  - Offline LAN worlds don't load skins, so the bot looks like a default
    player.
  - Tested on Minecraft 1.21.9 only.
  - Priorities are set in code, not in a settings file.
